Musical classification

El menú Classificació brings together the criteria OSAP uses to organise works. Each entry lets you explore the catalogue from a different musical perspective and review the values used.

What is it for? Classification helps you find repertoire by historical context, music type, catalogue references, instrumentation or ensemble. Not every work necessarily has all these details; missing or uncertain assignments may reflect incomplete source information.

1. Periods

Shows the historical periods used to place works in time. Names and chronological boundaries are indicative: musical periods overlap, and there is not always a universally accepted date separating them.

Possible issues: a work may fall between periods; its composition date may be unknown or disputed, and its period does not always coincide exactly with its composer's lifetime. If a classification seems incorrect, identify the work and provide a source supporting the correction.

2. Genres

Groups works by musical type or family. Genre describes a musical characteristic or tradition; it should not necessarily be confused with the work’s form, period or performing ensemble.

Possible issues: some works combine genres, names vary by tradition or source, and the same term can have different historical meanings. The available classification may be more general than a specialist's description.

3. Catalogues

Brings together numbering systems and thematic references used to identify works within a catalogue, such as BWV, Köchel or Hoboken. A catalogue reference helps distinguish works with the same title and recognise alternative names.

Possible issues: a number may vary between catalogue editions or revisions; a work may have more than one reference, and an imported reference may be incomplete or incorrectly transcribed. Check the full abbreviation and numbering, not just the number.

4. Instruments

Organises instrumentation and voice information associated with works. It lets you browse repertoire by instrument or instrument family when those details are recorded.

Possible issues: a source may describe incomplete scoring, use synonyms, group instruments differently, or confuse the original instrument with a later adaptation. An instrument's absence does not by itself prove that the work does not use it.

5. Ensembles

Describes vocal or instrumental groups, such as a choir, quartet, orchestra or other combinations. It refers to the ensemble for which the music is intended, which may differ from a single instrument.

Possible issues: a work may have several versions for different ensembles; ensemble names are not always equivalent, and some sources describe scoring in detail while others give only a general category.

How to interpret an uncertain classification

  1. Open the specific work and check which value is assigned.
  2. Distinguish missing data from a value that appears incorrect.
  3. If available, consult the original source or a reliable edition.
  4. Envía la incidencia desde Friends and music community, stating the work, the current value, the proposed value and the supporting evidence.

Classification reflects the data OSAP has been able to gather. Information is not complete for every work, and categories may need review.
