Works, Composers and Catalogue

Learn how to interpret work records, representations of a work, and information about authors and classifications.

1. A work and a representation are not the same

A work is the musical entity being identified. A representation is a specific edition, arrangement, score or version that a source associates with the work. One work can have several representations, each offering a different format.

Work

Shared musical identity: title, attributed authorship and catalogue data.

Representation

A specific version or edition, with source title, provider and format.

Resource

A file or link associated with a representation, when provided by the source.

2. Work record

The record brings together information that OSAP has been able to link across sources. Depending on the record, you may find:

  • Summary: main title and identifying details.
  • Representations: available versions, provider, format and available actions.
  • Evidence: signals explaining why records have been linked.
  • Relationships: aliases, catalogue references or other documented relationships.
  • Confidence: an estimate of how reliable the consolidation is. Treat it as guidance, not an absolute guarantee.

Use the available actions to view a score, download a resource where permitted, or open the original source page. Some actions work only when the provider is operational and the resource is accessible.

3. Composer profile

The composer index supports name searches. A person’s profile may include a biography, notable works, references, aliases and identity evidence. Not every field is available for every person.

If two profiles appear to refer to the same person, or contain clearly incorrect data, report the case through Contact and corrections. Merge and maintenance functions are reserved for administrators.

4. Musical classification

The Classification menu brings together the criteria OSAP uses to classify works. Each section has its own index and is explained with examples on the musical classification page.

Classification reflects the information available in the catalogue; some works may be unclassified, have incomplete data or require review.

5. Similar works or uncertain matches

When OSAP finds several potentially matching works, it may show candidates. Compare the title, author, catalogue, provider and evidence. Do not assume two results are identical just because their titles share a few words.

If the application cannot identify a work with sufficient confidence, it is better to preserve the uncertainty and report it than to assume an incorrect match.
